# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Two groups sharing one system.
- **Patients**: adults, often tired, anxious or low, using the app at home on a phone or laptop before a mental health appointment. They talk (or type) with a companion that interviews them, then review a plain-language report before anything reaches their clinician. Their job: say what is going on without having to repeat it to every new provider, and stay in control of what is shared.
- **Clinicians**: busy psychiatrists, therapists and care coordinators, often at a desk between appointments. Their job: triage a queue, read a structured pre-visit summary with evidence, spot risk fast, and follow up.

## Product Purpose
Patient Voice Companion collects a patient's story in conversation, screens with PHQ-9 and GAD-7 (scores computed in code, never by a model), and produces a report the patient approves before the clinician sees it. It screens and summarizes; it never diagnoses or advises treatment. Success: patients feel heard and in control; clinicians get a trustworthy, scannable summary and see imminent risk immediately.

## Brand Personality
Quiet, human, trustworthy. Unhurried and plain-spoken, like a well-run therapy practice or a good paper journal. Warmth comes from wording, type and restraint, not from decoration.

## Anti-references
- Generic wellness app: pastel gradients, rounded blobs, meditation-app serenity, stock illustrations.
- Corporate SaaS dashboard: identical stat cards, default blue-and-white, icon-heading-text grids.
- (Inferred, confirm) Hospital EHR clutter and anything alarming for a patient (harsh red outside real crisis moments).
- Cream/beige "warm" backgrounds as a shortcut to calm.

## Design Principles
1. **Interpreter, not lawyer**: show exactly what the patient said; nothing embellished.
2. **Nothing about me without me**: patient control (fix, don't share, approve) is always visible and unambiguous.
3. **Calm by structure, not decoration**: whitespace, type scale and clear single actions create calm; no ornamental graphics.
4. **Risk is unmistakable but never theatrical**: crisis and red-flag states are the only places strong color appears, and they are impossible to miss.
5. **Screen and summarize, never diagnose**: copy never labels, promises outcomes, or sounds clinical at the patient.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA minimum (assumed; confirm). Large base type for patients, keyboard reachable everything, focus trapped in the crisis overlay, aria-live for new messages, reduced-motion respected, color never the sole carrier of meaning (risk levels also use words/icons). Plain language at roughly a 6th-8th grade reading level for patient copy.
